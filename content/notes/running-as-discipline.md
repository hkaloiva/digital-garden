# Running as discipline

I find time for running and meditation most days. Not because I'm disciplined in the abstract — because without them, the rest of the system degrades. Running is less about fitness than about keeping the rest of me honest.

## What it actually does

- It burns off the stress that would otherwise leak into decisions and relationships.
- It creates a gap between stimulus and response — the space where judgment lives instead of reaction.
- It's a daily proof that I can keep a small promise to myself, which makes keeping promises to others easier.

## The honesty

I'm not an athlete. Some weeks are better than others. The point isn't perfection; it's that I notice when I stop, and I restart. Missing a week and not pretending I didn't is the real discipline.

## Why "discipline" and not "exercise"

Calling it exercise makes it optional and optimizable. Calling it discipline makes it structural — like the evening ritual or the kept promise. It's part of the operating system, not a nice-to-have. See [[Tight-loose-tight]].

## The link to work

Service delivery is stress-heavy. If I don't process it somewhere, it comes out in the wrong place — at home, or in a sharp reply to a client. Running is where I process it before it becomes someone else's problem. See [[Running and meditation]].
