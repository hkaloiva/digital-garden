# Context before verdict

My communication style: give the facts and the context first — "historically it was always http" — and only then the conclusion. Not "no." The history matters more than the verdict, because the verdict is only credible once the other person sees how I got there.

## Why context first

- A bare verdict asks for trust I haven't earned in that moment.
- Context lets the other person check my reasoning, not just my conclusion.
- It turns disagreement into a discussion about facts, not personalities.

## Where I learned it

On a client concern that was genuinely minor, my first instinct was to explain why it didn't matter. Instead I replied tactfully — no accusations, no dismissal — and checked the position with someone who knew the account's history. The concern stayed small. The trust stayed intact. See [[Tact is not evasion]].

## The tact connection

Tact is not evasion. Context before verdict is how I stay tactful without soft-pedalling: I name the problem, but I name it with the history attached. Evasion hides the problem; context-first communication names it in a way the other person can hear.

## The test

If I give a verdict and the other person can't reconstruct how I reached it, I've given a verdict, not communication. If they can, even if they disagree, we're talking about the same thing.
