# Tact is not evasion

Tact is not evasion. It's not soft-pedalling bad news or agreeing to keep the peace. Tact is stating the truth in a way the other person can actually hear it.

## The distinction

- Evasion hides the problem. Tact names it, but names it with respect.
- Evasion protects me from discomfort. Tact protects the relationship while still moving the issue forward.
- Evasion sounds like "it's fine." Tact sounds like "this is minor, and here's how we'll handle it."

## Where I learned this

A client raised a concern that was genuinely small. My first instinct was to explain why it didn't matter. Instead I replied tactfully — no accusations, no dismissal — and checked the position with someone who knew the account's history. The concern stayed small. The trust stayed intact.

## Why it matters for a Dealmaker

Relationships are the moat. But a relationship built on avoided truths is a liability, not an asset. Tact lets me keep the relationship and the truth at the same time.

## The test

If I'm being "tactful" and the other person still doesn't know there's a problem, I'm evading. If they know and feel respected, I'm being tactful.
