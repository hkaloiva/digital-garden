# The exit from the hamster wheel

The hamster wheel isn't failure — it's being limited without choice, running hard and going nowhere. The exit isn't quitting. It's converting competence into freedom, deliberately, before competence converts me into the system.

## What the exit looks like

- Build systems around relationships, not just effort — so the client doesn't need me in every conversation.
- Share the load so "only you can do this" stops meaning "only you do this."
- Convert service into product so revenue isn't tied to my hours.
- Keep a buffer so a bad month doesn't force a bad decision.

## Why it's hard

Competence is the trap. Being good at the job makes me the default for everything, and "only you can do this" becomes "only you do this." The same reliability that built trust can build a cage. See [[The hamster wheel]].

## What it's for

Freedom isn't for me alone. It's to share with friends, family, and my children. The point of the exit is being free to choose — and choosing them. See [[Evening ritual with the kids]].

## The honest gap

I say I want to start a business but "I don't have a clear goal and I'm not a businessman." The filter [[Interesting problems that help many people]] gives direction; the next step is picking one problem and going deep. See [[service-first-productize-later|Service first, productize later]].
