# Flat-fee vs hourly

I prefer flat-fee over hourly. Hourly rewards slowness; flat-fee rewards knowing what you're doing.

## Why flat-fee

- The client buys an outcome, not my time. That's cleaner for both sides.
- It forces me to scope properly. Scope creep on hourly is invisible; on flat-fee it's my problem.
- It decouples my income from my hours — the first step toward not being the hamster wheel.

## When hourly still makes sense

- Genuinely undefined scope where neither side can estimate.
- Advisory retainers where the value is ongoing judgment, not a deliverable.
- Early in a relationship, before trust exists.

## The Dealmaker angle

High-ticket flat-fee is a relationship sale. The price signals confidence. Discounting signals doubt. If I believe the outcome is worth it, I price it like I believe it.

## The trap

Flat-fee without a clear scope is a donation. Write the scope, the exclusions, and the change process before quoting. See [[A kept promise is success]] — a flat fee is still a promise.
