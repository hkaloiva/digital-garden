# Measuring by promises kept

How do I know if I'm doing well? Not by hours worked, not by tickets closed, not by praise. By promises kept — the ones I made to clients, to colleagues, to my kids, to myself.

## Why this metric

- It's objective in a way feelings aren't. Either I delivered what I said by when I said, or I didn't.
- It's cumulative. One kept promise is nothing; a thousand is a reputation.
- It's mine. Nobody else defines it for me, so I can't hide behind someone else's scorecard.

## Where it shows up

- In service delivery: every ticket, every SLA, every status update is a small promise.
- In life: the evening ritual with my kids is a promise I don't break.
- In the garden: publishing a note I said I'd publish. See [[Learning in public]].

## The tension

Measuring by promises kept can become a trap — saying yes to everything to be reliable, then drowning. The counter-principle: draw the line. Being reliable doesn't mean being available for everything. See [[Drawing the line between delivery and technical work]].

## The cost of a miss

A broken promise doesn't disappear; it becomes a debt I carry endlessly. That's the real price, and it's why I'd rather renegotiate early than miss silently. See [[Promise debt]].
