# Interesting problems

What makes a problem interesting to me isn't novelty or cleverness. It's the combination of three things: it's genuinely hard, it helps many people, and I can see a path where my specific experience makes a difference.

## The three tests

- Is it hard in a way that rewards thinking, not just effort? Boring work burns me out; hard work keeps me engaged.
- Does it help many people? I don't want to optimize ad clicks or shave milliseconds off something nobody cares about.
- Can I actually move it? A problem I care about but can't influence is just anxiety.

## Where it points me

Service delivery + knowledge management + AI is a problem I find genuinely interesting, affecting thousands of support teams. It's hard, it helps people, and it's exactly the experience I have. See [[Interesting problems that help many people]].

## What it rules out

- Faceless SaaS and volume plays.
- Things that strip the human out of the loop.
- Problems that are interesting but only for me — cleverness without reach.

## The gap

I say I want to start a business but "I don't have a clear goal and I'm not a businessman." The filter gives direction; the next step is picking one problem and going deep, not chasing the next shiny one. See [[The Dealmaker archetype]].
