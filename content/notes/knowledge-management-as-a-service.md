# Knowledge management as a service

I rebuilt the entire knowledge base for my client — technical documentation, end to end. That was a promise kept, and it taught me that **knowledge management is a service, not a side task**.

## The insight

Companies deploying AI support agents are failing at the data layer. Their knowledge bases are:

- Scattered across tools (Confluence, SharePoint, Slack, people's heads)
- Contradictory (two docs say different things)
- Written for humans, not for AI retrieval

Same root cause produces two pains:

1. **New agents/engineers can't find what they need** → slow ramp time (3+ months)
2. **AI support agents pull from the broken KB** → wrong answers, eroded trust

One problem, two buyers, one offer. See [[AI in support teams]].

## The opportunity

A 2–3 week **Knowledge Audit**: interview agents, sample real tickets where people got stuck, map KB gaps and contradictions, deliver a prioritized fix list + proposed structure. Enterprise/mid-market budget. Not a $500 gig.

## Distribution

Content-first: write about "why new hires take 3 months to ramp" and "why your AI agent keeps giving wrong answers." Same root cause, same buyer. Build authority before building product. Sell before you build.
