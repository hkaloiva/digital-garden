# Promise debt

A broken promise doesn't disappear. It becomes a debt I carry — mentally, emotionally, and inside the relationship it was made in. I think about it endlessly. That's the cost, and it's why I treat a promise as a debt in the first place.

## What the debt feels like

- It sits in the background of every interaction with that person afterwards.
- It erodes the one asset I actually have: trust.
- It compounds. One slip is recoverable; a pattern becomes a reputation.

## Why I'm like this

I got here from the bottom — bartender on low pay, penniless, wasting time. The thing that pulled me out was deciding a promise is a debt and a debt gets paid. So a broken one isn't a minor lapse; it's a betrayal of the thing that saved me.

## The trap

Keeping promises can become a trap if I say yes to everything to be reliable, then drown. The counter-principle is [[Drawing the line between delivery and technical work]]: being reliable doesn't mean being available for everything. Under-promise, over-deliver, and name the slip early.

## The rule

Give a deadline with a date. If it slips, say so immediately — I carry broken promises endlessly, and so does the other person. The cost of an early, honest renegotiation is tiny next to the cost of a silent miss.
