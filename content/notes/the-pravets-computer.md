# The Pravets computer

The Pravets was my first computer — a Bulgarian machine, the kind you don't see anymore. At an internal interview I showed a photo of it. It was a small thing, but it carried the whole argument.

## Why a photo beats a claim

- "I'm passionate about technology" is a claim. A photo of a real machine from my childhood is evidence.
- It said who I am, not just what I can do.
- Interviews blur; stories stick. I became memorable without performing.

## The deeper lesson: personal narrative as argument

Transferable skills are real, but they need a vehicle. I'd moved from customer service into an SDM role, and the qualities I'd built serving people genuinely transferred — but only once I gave them a concrete carrier. The photo was the carrier for "I've always been around computers, and I know how to serve people."

## How I use this now

- In sales and conversations: show, don't tell. A concrete example beats a capability list.
- In content: lead with the story, then extract the principle — which is what this garden is for.
- When hiring or advising: ask for the Pravets — the small, specific thing that proves the general claim.

## The caution

A story can become a crutch. If I lean on the Pravets forever, I'm trading on nostalgia instead of current competence. The photo opened a door; what I do after walking through it is what matters. See [[Learning in public]].
