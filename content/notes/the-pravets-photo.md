# The Pravets photo

At an internal interview, I showed a photo of my old Pravets computer. It was a small thing, but it carried the whole argument: I've been into this since before it was a career, and the qualities I built in customer service transfer.

## Why it worked

- It was concrete. Not "I'm passionate about technology" — a photo of a real machine from my childhood.
- It was personal. It said who I am, not just what I can do.
- It made me memorable. Interviews blur; stories stick.

## The lesson

Transferable skills are real, but they need a vehicle. The photo was the vehicle for "I know how to serve people, and I've always been around computers." Without it, the claim is just a claim.

## How I use this now

- In sales: show, don't tell. A concrete example beats a capability list.
- In content: lead with the story, then extract the principle.
- In hiring conversations: ask for the Pravets — the small, specific thing that proves the general claim.
