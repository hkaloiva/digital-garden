# Cross-checking is not weakness

I reach the answer myself first, then cross-check it with someone who knows the history. That's not indecision. That's how I avoid being wrong in a way that costs a client.

## The method

1. Work the problem alone. Form my own view.
2. Take it to someone who has seen this before — a former boss, a mentor, a colleague who worked the account.
3. Ask: "does this hold up?" Not "what should I do?"
4. Decide with the cross-check, not because of it.

## Why it isn't weakness

Weakness is outsourcing the decision. Cross-checking is stress-testing a decision I already own. The first is "tell me what to do." The second is "here's what I think — poke holes in it."

## Where it comes from

I like solving problems myself and not relying on anyone. That's a strength. But on delicate decisions, the person who worked the account before me knows the history I don't. Ignoring that to feel self-sufficient would be ego, not independence.

## The risk

Cross-checking can become a crutch if I only do it when I'm unsure and never when I'm confident. The discipline: cross-check the important calls, not the comfortable ones.
