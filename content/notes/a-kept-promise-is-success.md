# A kept promise is success

My operating law: **success = a promise kept.**

I got here from the bottom — bartender on low pay, penniless, wasting time. The thing that pulled me out wasn't talent or luck. It was deciding that a promise is a debt, and a debt gets paid.

## What this means in practice

- Give a deadline with a date. If it slips, say so immediately — I carry broken promises endlessly.
- Don't over-promise to look good. Under-promise, over-deliver.
- The client's trust is the asset. Everything else is a tool.

## Where it shows up

- In service delivery: every ticket, every SLA, every status update is a small promise.
- In life: the evening ritual with my kids (bedtime story) is a promise I don't break.

## The tension

Keeping promises can become a trap — saying yes to everything to be reliable, then drowning. The counter-principle: **draw the line**. Being reliable doesn't mean being available for everything. See [[Drawing the line between delivery and technical work]].
