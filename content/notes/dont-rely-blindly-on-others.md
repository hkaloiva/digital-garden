# Don't rely blindly on others

I like solving problems myself and not relying on anyone. That's a strength, not a flaw. But "not relying on anyone" can quietly become "refusing help to feel self-sufficient" — and that's ego, not independence.

## The distinction

- Independence: I work the problem, form my view, own the decision.
- Blind reliance: outsourcing the decision to someone else's authority.
- The trap: refusing input to protect the feeling of self-sufficiency.

## How I balance it

I reach the answer myself first, then cross-check with someone who knows the history — a former boss, a mentor, a colleague who worked the account. "Here's what I think — poke holes in it," not "what should I do?" See [[Cross-checking is not weakness]].

## Why this matters for a Dealmaker

Relationships are the moat, but I'm the one who has to own the call. A decision I can't explain is a decision I don't own. Cross-checking stress-tests a decision I already own; it doesn't replace ownership.

## The risk

Cross-checking can become a crutch if I only do it when unsure and never when confident. The discipline: cross-check the important calls, not the comfortable ones. And never let "I'll ask them" replace "I'll think first."
